## Topic T1A. Purpose and permissible use of the Amateur Radio Service; Operator/primary station license grant; Meanings of basic terms used in FCC rules; Interference; RACES rules; Phonetics; Frequency Coordinator; Beacon

The amateur service is a voluntary, noncommercial radio service regulated in the US by the FCC under Part 97. These questions cover its purpose, a few key definitions, interference, RACES, phonetics, frequency coordinators and beacons.

- Part 97's purposes include emergency communications, advancing the radio art, building operators' communication and technical skills, training operators and technicians, and international goodwill. ([47 CFR §97.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.1))
- A space station is any amateur station more than 50 km above the Earth's surface. ([47 CFR §97.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.3))
- Frequency coordinators recommend channels for repeater and auxiliary stations. Local amateurs recognize them; the FCC doesn't appoint them. ([47 CFR §97.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.3))
- Willful or malicious interference is prohibited. ([47 CFR §97.101](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.101))
- A RACES control operator needs an FCC amateur license and certification of enrollment in a civil defense organization. ([47 CFR §97.407](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.407))
- Phonetics are encouraged, not required, when identifying by voice. ([47 CFR §97.119](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.119))
- On HF, beacons may run under automatic control only between 28.20 and 28.30 MHz. ([47 CFR §97.203](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.203))

Part 97 opens with five principles behind the amateur service. One is advancing operators' skills in both the communication and technical phases of the radio art. Serving as many citizens as possible and international contesting aren't among them.

In the United States the FCC regulates the amateur service through Part 97 of its rules, and FCC representatives may inspect amateur stations. The ARRL is a private national organization of amateurs, not a regulator.

- [47 CFR §97.103](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.103): “(c) The station licensee must make the station and the station records available for inspection upon request by an FCC representative.”

## Topic T1B. Frequency allocations; Emission modes; Spectrum sharing; Transmissions near band edges; Contacting the International Space Station; Power output

Which frequencies, modes and power levels a Technician may use. Technicians have all amateur bands from 6 meters up, plus limited HF segments: CW on parts of 80, 40 and 15 meters, and CW, data and SSB voice on part of 10 meters.

- Technician HF segments: 3.525–3.600, 7.025–7.125, 21.025–21.200 and 28.0–28.5 MHz. ([47 CFR §97.301](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.301))
- On 80, 40 and 15 meters, Technicians may use CW only. ([47 CFR §97.307](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.307))
- On 10 meters, data is allowed from 28.0 to 28.3 MHz and phone from 28.3 to 28.5 MHz, where Technicians may use SSB voice. ([47 CFR §97.305](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.305))
- 6 meters is 50–54 MHz and 2 meters is 144–148 MHz in the US. ([47 CFR §97.301](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.301))
- 50.0–50.1 MHz is CW only, because other modes on 6 meters start at 50.1 MHz. ([47 CFR §97.305](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.305))
- Likewise 144.0–144.1 MHz is CW only, because other modes on 2 meters start at 144.1 MHz. ([47 CFR §97.305](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.305))
- Where amateurs are secondary, they must not interfere with primary users and must accept interference from them. ([47 CFR §97.303](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.303))
- Your signal, including its sidebands, must stay inside your band or segment, so don't set your frequency right at the edge. ([47 CFR §97.307](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.307))
- Technicians are limited to 200 W PEP in their HF segments. ([47 CFR §97.313](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.313))
- Above 30 MHz the limit is 1500 W PEP, apart from some band-specific limits. ([47 CFR §97.313](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.313))
- Always use the minimum power needed. ([47 CFR §97.313](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.313))

### T1B09

Your whole signal, not just the carrier frequency, must stay inside your privileges. Sidebands spread on either side of the dial frequency, and displays can be miscalibrated and transmitters can drift. So stay a little way inside the edge.

## Topic T1D. Authorized and prohibited transmissions; Sale of equipment

What amateurs may and may not transmit. The service is noncommercial and two-way: no broadcasting, no business, no codes or ciphers, and no music, with a few narrow exceptions.

- No contacts with countries whose administration has told the ITU it objects to them. ([47 CFR §97.111](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.111))
- Broadcasting (transmissions intended for the general public) is prohibited. ([47 CFR §97.113](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.113))
- Some one-way transmissions are allowed: brief tests and adjustments, telecommand, emergency communications, Morse practice, information bulletins and telemetry. ([47 CFR §97.111](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.111))
- No messages encoded to obscure their meaning, except special codes used to command a space station. ([47 CFR §97.211](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.211))
- Control signals for model craft aren't considered codes meant to obscure meaning. ([47 CFR §97.215](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.215))
- No obscene or indecent language. ([47 CFR §97.113](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.113))
- No music, except incidental music in authorized retransmissions of manned spacecraft communications. ([47 CFR §97.113](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.113))
- No pay for operating, with narrow exceptions such as teachers using a station in classroom instruction. ([47 CFR §97.113](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.113))
- You may occasionally advertise amateur gear for sale or trade, but not on a regular basis. ([47 CFR §97.113](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.113))
- Model craft control signals don't need on-air identification if the transmitter carries a label with your call sign, name and address. ([47 CFR §97.215](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.215))

## Topic T1E. Control operator: eligibility, designating, privileges, duties, location, required; Control point; Control types: automatic, remote

Every amateur station needs a control operator when it transmits. The control operator is responsible for the station following the rules, and their license class decides the station's privileges.

- A station must have a control operator whenever it transmits. ([47 CFR §97.7](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.7))
- The station licensee designates the control operator and is presumed to be the control operator unless records show otherwise. ([47 CFR §97.103](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.103))
- When the control operator isn't the licensee, both are equally responsible. ([47 CFR §97.103](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.103))
- The station operates with the privileges of the control operator's license class. ([47 CFR §97.9](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.9))
- The control point is where the control operator function is performed. ([47 CFR §97.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.3))
- Local and remote control need the control operator at the control point; any station may be remotely controlled, for example over the internet. ([47 CFR §97.109](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.109))
- Under automatic control (repeaters, for example) the control operator need not be at the control point. ([47 CFR §97.109](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.109))

**D. Never**  

Every amateur station must have a control operator whenever it transmits. Even under automatic control, such as a repeater, there is still a control operator; they just don't need to be at the control point.

## Topic T1F. Station identification; Repeaters; Third party communications; Club stations; FCC inspection

Station identification, repeaters, third-party traffic, club stations and FCC inspections.

- Give your call sign at least every 10 minutes during a communication and at the end of it, even when using tactical calls. ([47 CFR §97.119](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.119))
- Identify by voice in English, or by CW. Phonetics are encouraged. ([47 CFR §97.119](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.119))
- Indicators are separated from your call sign by a slant mark or a word that means it, like "stroke", "slant" or "slash". ([47 CFR §97.119](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.119))
- Third-party communications are messages sent on behalf of another person. ([47 CFR §97.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.3))
- International third-party traffic is allowed only with countries that have an arrangement with the US, apart from emergency or disaster relief traffic. ([47 CFR §97.115](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.115))
- A repeater retransmits another station on a different channel. Its control operator isn't accountable for rule violations it passes along inadvertently. ([47 CFR §97.205](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.205))
- A club station license requires a club of at least four people. ([47 CFR §97.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.5))
- The licensee must make the station and its records available whenever an FCC representative asks. ([47 CFR §97.103](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.103))
